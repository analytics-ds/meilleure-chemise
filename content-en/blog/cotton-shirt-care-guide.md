> **In brief:**
> 1. Cotton shrinks by 3 to 5% per hot tumble-drying cycle, compared to near-zero shrinkage when air-dried.
> 2. The recommended washing temperature is between 30 and 40°C to preserve color and fiber structure.
> 3. An iron can handle the "cotton" setting (150 to 200°C depending on the model), a high temperature that makes ironing easier than linen or silk.
> 4. Storing shirts on a hanger, rather than folded in a drawer, prevents permanent creases at the shoulders and chest.

## Why care determines a shirt's lifespan

A well cared-for cotton shirt lasts several years of regular wear, while poor care (washing too hot, aggressive drying, badly calibrated ironing) can visibly damage it within months. Cotton is a sturdy natural fiber, but sensitive to three specific factors: water temperature, drying heat, and repeated exposure to machine-wash friction.

### What damages a cotton shirt fastest

- Washing at high temperature (60°C and above), which weakens the fibers and promotes shrinkage
- High-heat tumble drying, the main cause of cotton shrinkage
- Overly aggressive spin cycles, which set deep creases that are harder to iron out afterward

## Washing: the right temperature and cycle

### The right temperature

For a classic cotton shirt, a temperature of **30 to 40°C** is enough in the vast majority of cases and preserves both color and fiber structure. A higher temperature (60°C) should be reserved for heavily stained white shirts, keeping in mind that it accelerates fabric wear over time.

### The cycle and products

A delicate or "shirt" cycle (when the machine offers it) limits the mechanical friction responsible for premature fiber wear and color fading. On the product side, liquid detergent rather than powder reduces the risk of visible whitish residue on dark fabrics, particularly around the collar.

## Drying: the real cause of shrinkage

Drying is the most decisive factor for a cotton shirt's longevity, even more so than washing.

### Why tumble drying shrinks cotton

Cotton fiber contracts under the combined effect of heat and the drum's mechanical motion. A high-heat tumble dryer can shrink a cotton shirt by **3 to 5% per cycle**, a cumulative effect that, repeated over several washes, eventually makes the shirt visibly smaller and the collar tighter.

### The recommended method

Air drying, on a hanger rather than flat, keeps shrinkage close to zero and lets the fabric's natural weight smooth out some of the creases even before ironing. This is the preferred method for quality shirts.

### Drying method comparison table

| Method | Estimated shrinkage | Effect on wrinkling | Duration |
|--------|----------------------|----------------------|----------|
| High-heat tumble dry | 3 to 5% per cycle | Heavy wrinkling | Fast (30-45 min) |
| Low-heat tumble dry | 1 to 2% per cycle | Moderate wrinkling | Medium (45-60 min) |
| Air dry on hanger | Near zero | Low, fabric smooths naturally | Long (several hours) |
| Air dry flat | Near zero | Medium, risk of distortion if laid badly | Long |

## Ironing: temperature and technique

### The right iron temperature

Cotton tolerates a relatively high iron temperature, generally corresponding to the **"cotton"** setting on most irons (between 150 and 200°C depending on the model). It is one of the easiest fabrics to iron, unlike linen, which often needs a more moderate temperature combined with more steam to avoid shining the fabric.

### The technique for a crisp result

1. **Iron the shirt while still slightly damp**, straight out of the wash or lightly sprayed, for more effective de-creasing with less effort
2. **Start with the collar and cuffs**, the stiffest parts, before moving to the sleeves and then the back and front
3. **Iron sleeves flat, seam at the edge**, to avoid setting an unsightly double crease
4. **Finish with the body of the shirt**, gently pulling the fabric taut to remove remaining creases

## Storage: the often overlooked step

Poor storage can undo the benefits of careful washing and ironing within a few weeks.

### Hanger rather than folding

Storing shirts on a **hanger**, ideally one with shaped shoulders rather than plain wire, avoids the permanent creases at the shoulders and chest caused by prolonged folding in a drawer. This matters particularly for shirts worn in a professional context, where a crisp look is expected from the start of the day.

### What a textile durability study points out

> "The average lifespan of a natural fiber garment depends more on care conditions (washing temperature, drying method) than on the fabric's intrinsic quality at purchase."
> — ADEME, textile lifespan study, 2022

This directly ties back to care habits: a high-end cotton poplin shirt poorly cared for will wear out faster than a standard shirt properly washed and dried.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>How do you care for a cotton shirt so it lasts?</summary>

Wash at 30-40°C on a delicate cycle, never tumble dry (cotton shrinks by 3 to 5% per hot drying cycle), iron while still slightly damp to reduce effort and the risk of scorching, and store on a hanger rather than folded to avoid permanent creases. These four habits significantly extend a cotton shirt's lifespan.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What temperature should you use to iron a cotton shirt?</summary>

Cotton tolerates a high iron temperature, generally the "cotton" setting (150-200°C depending on the iron), which makes it an easy fabric to iron. It is best to iron the shirt while still slightly damp, straight out of the wash or lightly sprayed, for a crisp result with less effort.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why does a cotton shirt yellow over time?</summary>

Yellowing usually comes from a build-up of sweat, deodorant and poorly rinsed detergent residue around the collar and underarms, combined with natural fiber oxidation from light exposure. Regular washing at the right temperature and a full rinse cycle limit this effect.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can a cotton shirt go in the tumble dryer?</summary>

It is not recommended if you want to preserve the shirt's size and shape: cotton can shrink by 3 to 5% per hot drying cycle. Air drying on a hanger remains the safest method.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How long does a well cared-for cotton shirt last?</summary>

A quality cotton poplin shirt, cared for following best practices (washing at 30-40°C, air drying, proper ironing), generally lasts several years of regular wear without noticeable distortion.

</details>
