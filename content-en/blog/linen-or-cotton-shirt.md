> **In brief:**
> 1. Linen absorbs up to 20% of its weight in moisture before feeling damp, compared to about 7% for cotton, which makes it feel cooler to wear.
> 2. Cotton wrinkles noticeably less than linen thanks to a more elastic fiber, and keeps a crisp look throughout the day.
> 3. Quality linen weighs between 130 and 170 g/m2 (combed linen); premium cotton poplin sits around 100 to 120 g/m2 with a twisted yarn.
> 4. Linen-cotton blends (around 50/50) reduce linen's wrinkling while keeping part of its breathability.

## Linen and cotton: two natural fibers, two different logics

A linen shirt and a cotton shirt start from two distinct plant fibers. **Linen** comes from the stem of the flax plant (Linum usitatissimum), grown mainly in North-Western Europe (France, Belgium, the Netherlands), which accounts for most of the world's high-quality textile flax production. **Cotton** comes from the fiber surrounding the cottonseed, grown in warmer regions (Egypt, India, the United States, West Africa).

This different origin already explains part of their properties: linen fiber is longer, stiffer and less elastic than cotton fiber, which directly affects how the shirt behaves in wear.

### A question of fiber structure

Under a microscope, linen fiber shows a hollow structure (central lumen) that helps air circulate and moisture escape. Cotton fiber is more twisted and shorter, which makes it softer to the touch but less effective at releasing body heat.

## Breathability and thermal comfort

This is the criterion that most clearly separates the two fabrics in use.

**Linen** is known for its moisture absorption: it can absorb up to **20% of its weight in water** before feeling damp to the touch, compared to about **7% for cotton**. In practice, a linen shirt wicks away sweat better and gives a noticeably cooler feeling once the temperature goes above 25°C (77°F).

**Cotton**, on the other hand, retains more body heat. That is an advantage in mid-season or air-conditioned interiors, but a drawback in full summer heat or on a hot, humid day.

### What this means day to day

- A linen shirt clings less to the skin even after several hours in strong heat
- A cotton shirt, especially in tight-weave poplin, offers better insulation against wind or air conditioning
- Linen dries faster than cotton after washing, an advantage while traveling

## Drape, wrinkling and overall look

Wrinkling is where cotton clearly wins.

Linen fiber, being less elastic, shows creases within the first few hours of wear. This is an accepted characteristic of the fabric: a linen shirt that stays perfectly smooth all day does not exist, barring a specific chemical treatment (washed linen, which softens the fiber but does not remove the wrinkling). Many brands actually present this natural creasing as a sign of the fabric's authenticity rather than a flaw.

**Cotton**, particularly in poplin weave (fine yarn, tight plain weave) or oxford (thicker yarn, twill weave), keeps a crisp drape for longer. It is the reference fabric for a business shirt that needs to stay impeccable all day.

### Linen vs cotton comparison table

| Criterion | Linen | Cotton |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| Moisture absorption | Up to 20% of weight in water | About 7% of weight in water |
| Wrinkling | Noticeable, characteristic of the fabric | Low to moderate depending on weave |
| Common weight (shirt) | 130 to 170 g/m2 (combed linen) | 100 to 130 g/m2 (poplin) |
| Drying time after washing | Fast | Slower |
| Best season for use | Spring and summer | Year round |
| Care | Steam ironing recommended | Easier to iron |

## Weight, weave and fabric quality

Fabric weight, expressed in grams per square meter (g/m2), is a direct indicator of quality and intended use.

For **linen**, combed linen (long aligned fibers, softer feel, 130 to 170 g/m2) is distinct from carded linen (shorter fibers, more rustic feel). Quality combed linen remains the reference for a dress shirt.

For **cotton**, the weave changes the texture more than the weight does: **poplin** (fine yarn, tight plain weave) gives a smooth, light surface, **oxford** (thicker yarn, 2x1 twill weave) gives a more textured, casual look, and **cotton gauze** (loose weave, finer yarn) offers a lightness close to linen with less wrinkling.

### What a reference study on European linen says

> "European linen stands out for its mechanical strength and its absorption capacity, higher than most natural fibers, which makes it a preferred material for summer clothing."
> — European Confederation of Linen and Hemp (CELC), 2023

This mechanical strength explains why a linen shirt, despite its creased look, holds up over time when properly cared for.

## How to choose based on use

The choice between linen and cotton depends mainly on the wearing context and tolerance for wrinkling.

### Questions to ask before buying

1. **The season**: linen is favored from spring through the end of summer, cotton is worn year round
2. **The professional context**: a formal office environment does not sit well with a heavily creased shirt; cotton (poplin in particular) remains the safer choice
3. **The climate**: in hot, humid regions, linen's breathability becomes a comfort criterion, not just a style choice
4. **How much ironing you are willing to do**: linen requires more regular care to stay presentable

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>Linen or cotton shirt: which should you choose?</summary>

Linen suits warm weather best because its hollow fiber absorbs up to 20% of its weight in moisture before feeling damp, compared to about 7% for cotton. Cotton, on the other hand, gives a crisper drape and wrinkles less, making it more versatile day to day and easier to iron. For a shirt worn year round, at the office or in the evening, cotton stays the safer pick. For summer or hot climates, linen is noticeably more comfortable.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is linen more fragile than cotton?</summary>

Dry linen fiber is actually stronger under tension than cotton, but it is less elastic, which makes it more prone to creasing and sharp wrinkles. Quality linen (combed linen, 130 to 170 g/m2) lasts for years with the right care.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can linen and cotton be blended in a shirt?</summary>

Yes, linen-cotton blends (often 50/50 or 55% linen / 45% cotton) combine linen's breathability with cotton's crisp hold, with a middle-ground level of wrinkling. It is a good compromise for a shirt worn across seasons.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Does linen shrink when washed?</summary>

Unwashed linen can shrink by 3 to 5% on its first hot-water wash. That is why most linen shirts sold today are pre-washed at the factory (washed linen) to stabilize the size before sale.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Which fabric should I choose for a year-round office shirt?</summary>

For regular professional use with little time for daily ironing, cotton poplin remains the most reliable choice. It keeps a crisp look longer than linen and irons more easily.

</details>
