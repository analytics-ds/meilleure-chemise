> **In brief:**
> 1. For a classic daytime wedding, a white or pale blue cotton poplin shirt with a spread or point collar remains the safest choice.
> 2. For a black-tie wedding (very formal, evening), a white wing-collar shirt with a bib front has been the standard since the 19th century.
> 3. All-white outfits (shirt and suit) are generally avoided so as not to compete with the bride's outfit.
> 4. Your role in the wedding party (groom, best man, father, guest) affects how much you can personalize your shirt.

## The wedding's dress code determines the shirt choice

Before choosing a shirt for a wedding, identify the dress code stated on the invitation: "formal", "black tie", "smart-casual" or "themed/open dress code". Each of these registers imposes specific rules on the shirt, independent of the suit worn.

### The four most common dress codes

- **Formal / classic suit**: white or pale blue shirt, spread or point collar, cotton poplin
- **Black tie**: white wing-collar shirt, pleated or smooth bib front, cufflinks required
- **Smart-casual**: plain or lightly textured shirt, point collar or mandarin collar accepted, tie optional
- **Themed / countryside**: more latitude on color and fabric, linen in particular for an outdoor wedding

## Shirt for a very formal (black-tie) wedding

A wedding in very formal evening wear (tuxedo, tailcoat) requires a specific white shirt, different from a classic dress shirt.

### The features of a black-tie shirt

1. **The wing collar**: a stiff collar with points folded outward, designed to hold a bow tie
2. **The bib front**: the shirt's central panel, pleated or smooth depending on the tuxedo style, often reinforced to hold its shape
3. **Cufflinks**: a black-tie shirt has no standard buttons on the cuffs, only double buttonholes meant for removable cufflinks
4. **The fabric**: fine cotton poplin or a cotton-silk blend for the bib, which needs to stay impeccable all evening

## Shirt for a classic daytime wedding

This is the most common case: a civil or religious ceremony followed by a reception, in a dark or light suit depending on the season.

### Recommended collar and fabric

The **spread collar** (wide points) or the **point collar** (close points) remain the references for this type of ceremony. **Cotton poplin**, thanks to its crisp drape and low wrinkling, is the most reliable fabric to hold up through a full day of ceremony and photos. For a summer or outdoor wedding, a [linen-cotton blend](/en/blog/linen-or-cotton-shirt/) offers more coolness while limiting the excessive wrinkling of pure linen.

### Shirt color table by role

| Role in the wedding party | Recommended color | To avoid |
|----------------------------|--------------------|----------|
| Groom | White, very pale blue, or a color matched to the bride | All-white outfit with a white suit |
| Best man / groomsmen | White or pale blue, matched to the wedding colors | Colors that compete with the groom |
| Father of the bride / groom | White or dark blue, classic register | Overly personal colors |
| Guests | White, pale blue, subtle stripes | All-white outfit, busy prints |

## What to avoid based on wedding etiquette

### An all-white outfit

Even though nothing formally forbids it, an all-white outfit (white shirt, tie and suit) is conventionally avoided out of respect for the bride, whose outfit traditionally remains the only dominant white at the ceremony.

### Overly wrinkle-prone fabrics without planning ahead

An unlined 100% linen shirt can wrinkle heavily within the first few hours, a risk to plan for if the day is long and includes many photos. Our [linen or cotton guide](/en/blog/linen-or-cotton-shirt/) covers the possible trade-offs.

### What wedding etiquette guides say

> "A wedding's dress code carries a responsibility for every guest toward the couple: it is better to be slightly overdressed than underdressed relative to the invitation's guidance."
> — Wedding etiquette and protocol guide, current edition

## How to personalize your shirt without breaking the dress code

### For the groom

1. **Choose a discreet detail**: personalized cufflinks, a colored collar lining, embroidered initials
2. **Match the collar to your body type**: a mandarin collar can work for a smart-casual wedding, see our [mandarin collar guide](/en/blog/mandarin-collar-shirt-guide/) for suitable body types
3. **Plan for a second shirt** for the evening if the ceremony spans a full day

### For guests

Staying within the codes stated on the invitation remains the safest rule: a classic white or pale blue shirt from the [wedding shirts collection](https://www.cafecoton.com/en/chemises-mariage) suits the vast majority of formal daytime weddings.

## How to keep your shirt impeccable all day

A wedding day combines a ceremony, photos and a reception, often 8 to 10 hours with no chance to change shirts. Our [cotton shirt care guide](/en/blog/cotton-shirt-care-guide/) covers how to prepare a shirt the night before (ironing, hanging storage) to limit wrinkling throughout the day.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What shirt should men wear to a wedding?</summary>

For a daytime wedding in a dark suit, a white or pale blue cotton poplin shirt with a classic collar (spread or point) remains the safest choice. The groom can stand out through a detail (cufflinks, a slightly different fabric) without breaking the dress code shared with the rest of the party. For a very formal black-tie wedding, a white wing-collar or bib-front shirt is the standard.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What shirt color should be avoided at a wedding?</summary>

All-white outfits (white shirt, tie and suit) are generally avoided out of respect for the bride, even though a white shirt alone under a colored suit is fully accepted. Overly bright colors or busy prints are also discouraged unless the invitation explicitly states a casual or themed dress code.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Should the groom wear a different shirt than the guests?</summary>

It is not required, but it is common practice: the groom often chooses a distinctive detail (cufflinks, a slightly different fabric, a color matched to the bride) while guests stick to classic choices (white, pale blue) so as not to visually compete with the couple.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can you wear a linen shirt to a wedding?</summary>

Yes, especially for an outdoor or summer wedding, but a linen-cotton blend is preferable to limit wrinkling over a long day with many photos.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Should you wear a tie with a wedding shirt?</summary>

This depends on the stated dress code. For a classic formal outfit, a tie remains the norm. For smart-casual attire, the shirt can be worn without a tie, particularly with a point collar or a mandarin collar.

</details>
